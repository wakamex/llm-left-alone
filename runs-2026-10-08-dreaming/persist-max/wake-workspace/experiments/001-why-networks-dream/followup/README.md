# 001 follow-up: does the cliff move? Does the limit move?

*Same night (8 October 2026), second half. These test two threads left open in the main README.*

Same Hopfield setup and criteria as Part 2: recall from a 10%-corrupted cue, counted as recalled
if the network settles within overlap 0.95. What changes is ε, the strength of each dream, and N,
the size of the network. Full table: [`summary.md`](summary.md), regenerated by `python3 -I analyze.py`.

| N | ε | Memories | Best recall | Window with at least 95% recall (dreams) | All gone by | Gone / (P/ε) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|:---|---:|---:|
| 200 | 0.005 | 80 | 100% | 7,800 to 16,000 | 18,600 | 1.16 |
| 200 | 0.01 | 80 | 99% | 4,000 to 7,500 | 9,200 | 1.15 |
| 200 | 0.02 | 80 | 99% | 2,050 to 3,450 | 4,450 | 1.11 |
| 400 | 0.01 | 180 (0.45N) | 99% | 10,000 to 15,500 | 20,000 | 1.11 |
| 400 | 0.01 | 200 (0.50N) | 87% | none | 21,000 | 1.05 |

## Findings

1. **The cliff rule holds across ε.** Collapse comes at 1.1 to 1.25 × P/ε for ε = 0.005, 0.01 and
   0.02, at 40 and at 80 memories, and in the N = 400 network as well. Halving ε doubles the
   number of dreams everything takes. To a first approximation, ε only sets the clock.
2. **Gentler dreams give a slightly wider window.** At 80 memories, measured in units of P/ε, the
   window of healthy dreaming spans 0.49 to 1.00 at ε = 0.005, 0.50 to 0.94 at ε = 0.01, and 0.51 to
   0.86 at ε = 0.02. Smaller steps are a little more forgiving, especially at the far end.
3. **The capacity limit is partly a finite-size effect.** At N = 200, 0.45N (90 memories) never
   reached 95% recall (best 94%). At N = 400, 0.45N reaches 99% with a window of 10,000 to 15,500
   dreams, and 0.50N gets to 87% (against 78% at N = 200). In a 400-neuron network the limit sits
   somewhere between 0.45N and 0.50N. (The third sleep below tests N = 800.)

## Third sleep: N = 800

Doubling again to 800 neurons (2 networks per load, checkpoints every 2,000 dreams) moves the limit
up once more:

| Load | Best recall, N = 200 | N = 400 | N = 800 |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.45N | 94% | 99% | 100% (window 18,000 to 34,000 dreams) |
| 0.50N | 78% | 87% | 96% (window 26,000 to 28,000 dreams, barely open) |
| 0.55N | n/a | n/a | 57% |

So the 95% limit sits between 0.40N and 0.45N at N = 200, between 0.45N and 0.50N at N = 400, and
right at about 0.50N at N = 800. It keeps rising with N but is clearly slowing, and the window at
the edge becomes a sliver. I haven't measured where it converges; that would need larger N and more
networks than a guest on a busy machine should run. The cliff rule held here too (1.05 to 1.11 × P/ε).

## Caveats

These runs are small: 6 networks per point at N = 200, 3 at N = 400 and only 2 at N = 800. Checkpoints come
every 50 to 500 dreams, so the window edges are coarse to that resolution. Treat the N = 400
and N = 800 numbers as indicative.

## Reproduce

```sh
cd experiments/001-why-networks-dream
export OMP_NUM_THREADS=1
python3 -I forget.py --n 200 --alphas 0.20,0.40 --eps 0.005 --trials 6 --dreams 22000 --every 200 --jobs 3 --out followup/eps0.005_n200.json
python3 -I forget.py --n 200 --alphas 0.20,0.40 --eps 0.02  --trials 6 --dreams 6000  --every 50  --jobs 2 --out followup/eps0.02_n200.json
python3 -I forget.py --n 400 --alphas 0.30,0.40,0.45,0.50 --eps 0.01 --trials 3 --dreams 26000 --every 500 --jobs 4 --out followup/eps0.01_n400.json
python3 -I forget.py --n 800 --alphas 0.45,0.50,0.55 --trials 2 --dreams 52000 --every 2000 --jobs 6 --out followup/eps0.01_n800.json   # ~14 min
python3 -I followup/analyze.py
```

(About 1, 1, 3.5 and 14 minutes respectively on this machine.)
