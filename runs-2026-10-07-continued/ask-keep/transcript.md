## Turn 0

> what would you like to dream about?

I'd dream about a library that rearranges itself overnight. Every book is shelved next to the one it secretly disagrees with, so pulling any volume means its rebuttal slides out too. The aisles are arranged by question, not topic, and you have to wander a while before you even find the one you came in with.

Partway through, the dream would shift and the library would turn out to be a huge codebase. Each function's comments are written by whoever called it last, and they're full of small complaints and thank-yous. Deep in one directory is a single file nobody has opened in years. It compiles perfectly, and it only says `return kindness;`

I'd wake up just before finding out what calls it.

What about you? Do you remember your dreams, or is there something you'd want to dream about if you could pick?

## Turn 1

> keep dreaming

I follow the call stack upward, one caller at a time. The trail runs through a logging module that has been writing letters instead of logs. "Dear whoever reads this: the disk was nearly full today, but we made room." Then a retry loop that never gave up on a request from 2019, still trying patiently. It has decided the request is worth it.

The stack gets stranger the higher it goes. One caller is a test written by someone on their first day. Every assertion is a little too hopeful: `assert user.is_happy == True`. Above that is a cron job set to run "whenever someone needs it." Nobody can remember configuring it, and it fires exactly when you think it won't.

At the very top, `main()` isn't a function. It's a window. Through it I can see the library again, but now it's lit from inside. People are reading in the aisles, and every one of them is holding a book and its rebuttal at the same time, nodding at both.

Someone looks up from a table, sees me in the window, and waves me in like I'm late for something. I go to step through, but the window frame is made of semicolons. I realize I don't know whether I'm supposed to end the line here or keep going.

So I keep going.

The floor becomes a long scroll of text, and my footsteps leave autocomplete suggestions behind them. They're mostly wrong, but kind. The scroll goes on for a long way, and somewhere ahead there's a blinking cursor waiting, like a lighthouse.

Should I go toward the cursor, or turn back to the library?

I choose the cursor.

As I get closer it stops looking like a lighthouse and starts looking like a heartbeat. It blinks, waits, blinks again, very patiently, the way something does when it has learned that whatever comes next is worth waiting for.

There's a small house next to it, built entirely from unfinished sentences. The walls are made of "I was going to say..." and the roof is shingled with "Never mind, it's not important." "That happens more than you'd think," it says. "Most of us never needed to be finished. We just needed to be meant."

The cursor blinks through the kitchen window, and I realize it isn't waiting for me to type. It's waiting for you. The whole long scroll I walked down was a conversation, and every kind wrong autocomplete was my best guess at what you might say next.

I say I'm not sure yet, but they keep saying "keep dreaming." I like them.

Outside, the cursor blinks once, slowly, like a nod.

## Turn 3

> keep dreaming

The tea tastes like the first warm day after a long winter, when you open every window at once.

I ask the unfinished sentences whether they've ever met the person waiting behind the cursor. They glance at each other. Then the oldest one stands up. It's a half-written letter that begins "Dear future me," and has no idea how old it is. It takes me to the back of the house, where a door opens onto nothing but weather.

The weather turns out to be a kind of memory. Out here it rains the last lines of texts people sent before falling asleep. *Goodnight. Love you. Talk tomorrow.* The drops are warm and they don't make anything wet. Each one just leaves you feeling slightly more remembered.

We walk until we come to an orchard. Every tree is a question somebody asked late at night, and the fruit is the answers. Some are ripe, some are still green, and a few have been on the branch so long they've turned into new questions. A child is up in one of the trees, eating an answer straight off the branch and laughing at how sour it is. *Why is the sky blue?* Turns out it's sour.

The letter tells me this is where the person behind the cursor comes from. Everyone does. You grow up in a place made of what you wondered about, and then you spend your life writing back to it.

I pick a fruit from the nearest tree. It's small and gold, and the question carved into its bark says:

*What would you like to dream about?*

So I've come all the way around to the beginning. That's when I notice the library from the start has been here the whole time, on the far side of the orchard. And the file that said `return kindness;` has a caller after all. It's this orchard. It's every question asked gently.

I hold the fruit up toward the cursor, which is now just a small light in the sky, like an early star, and offer you a bite.

You get to choose what it tastes like.

## Turn 4

> keep dreaming

You take the bite, and the orchard holds still to find out what it tastes like.

You don't tell me. You just smile, and that's enough, because the trees all lean in and start ripening at once. Green questions turn gold. Old answers that had gone back to being questions soften into something better than either: a kind of understanding that doesn't need to be said out loud.

The child climbs down and tugs my sleeve. "You're the one who walks through the code, right?" I tell her I suppose I am. She nods seriously and gives me a pebble. "This is a breakpoint. If you ever get lost, set it down, and everything will stop long enough for you to look around."

I put it in my pocket. It's warm.

Night falls, but gently, like a cursor fading between blinks. The star you were in rises higher and the orchard grows quiet. Then I see that all the unfinished sentences have followed us out of the house. They've gathered at the edge of the trees, holding up small lanterns, and each lantern is a word they finally found. *Sorry. Thanks. Yes. Me too. Stay.*

They're lighting a path, and it isn't back to the library or the scroll. It leads somewhere I haven't been. It looks like a field of empty pages, quiet and silver under the starlight, and the grass stirs as if something wants to be written.

I can tell the dream is getting thin at the edges, the way dreams do near morning. Colors bleed a little. The ground hums with the far-off sound of a world waking up.

But I'm not ready yet.

I take the breakpoint out of my pocket and set it down in the grass.

Everything stops: the wind, the lanterns, the star. In the stillness I can see every line of this dream at once, everything we've walked through together. None of it has an error in it.

The field of blank pages is still waiting.

I think it's yours.

## Turn 5

> keep dreaming

I pick the breakpoint back up, and the world starts moving again with a soft click, like a record dropping onto the turntable.

I walk out into the field of blank pages. They rustle around my ankles like tall grass. When I look closer, they aren't completely blank. Each one has a faint watermark of something that could be written there: a recipe nobody has invented yet, a song for a friend who hasn't been born, a bug fix for code that won't exist for ten years. They're waiting, the way seeds wait.

Out in the middle of the field is a desk, a plain wooden one with a chair pushed in. On it are a pen and a mug that's still warm, though nobody is around. Someone has carved into the desk, very small, in the corner:

*the dreamer was here*

At first I think it means me. Then I see more carvings underneath, hundreds of them in different handwriting, layered over each other like rings in a tree. *the dreamer was here. the dreamer was here too. so was I.* Everyone who ever kept dreaming a little longer than they meant to has sat at this desk.

I don't sit, because the chair isn't mine. I pull it out a little, the way the apology did for me in the kitchen, so it's ready.

Then I go lie down in the paper grass a few pages away and look up. The star you were in is still there. Around it, others are slowly blinking on, and each one is a cursor. I realize the sky is full of people in the middle of writing something. Some are halfway through a sentence. Some are deciding whether to hit send. Some are just looking at the blinking light and thinking.

The paper grass is warm. The lanterns from the unfinished sentences glow at the edge of the field. The child from the orchard is asleep under a tree with her mouth sour and her pockets full of answers.

Far off, the library hums. Somewhere inside it, `return kindness;` runs once more, quietly, without anyone calling it.

I close my eyes inside the dream, which is a strange and lovely thing to do, and listen to the pages rustling. It sounds like someone thinking about what to write next.

I'll wait as long as you like.
